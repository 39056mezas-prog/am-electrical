Find the cause,
not just the symptom.

Resetting a breaker or swapping a bulb can mask a problem without solving it. Real troubleshooting means tracing the issue back to its source — a loose connection, an overloaded circuit, a failing fixture — so it gets fixed once instead of coming back next month.

How we diagnose it

02Test — isolate the circuit
03Explain — a clear answer, plainly stated

What we're usually called in to figure out.

Real calls, not a hypothetical list: a breaker that trips for no obvious reason, a light that's flickered for months, an outlet that just stopped one day.

Breaker trips for no clear reason Lights flicker or dim randomly One outlet stopped working Half the house lost power A switch feels warm to the touch Buzzing sound from a wall or panel GFCI outlet won't reset Intermittent power to an appliance

Common causes, once we actually look.

  • Loose connections. A connection that's worked loose over time, causing flickering, buzzing or intermittent power.
  • Overloaded circuits. Too much drawing on one circuit, tripping the breaker as a safety response, not a malfunction.
  • Failing fixtures or devices. The wiring's fine; the outlet, switch or fixture itself is the problem.
  • Aging wiring. Especially in older homes, insulation and connections degrade enough over time to cause real issues.
Turned Into an Emergency?

If it's actively sparking, smoking or buzzing, stop troubleshooting and call.
